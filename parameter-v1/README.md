`parameters.csv` supplies Austin with SI parameters, an evidence category and source, and CARLA readback or measurement where known. Blank is unknown, not zero. Rows deliberately distinguish real-vehicle references, team inputs, derived values, and CARLA observations. Select a coherent scenario and approved effective friction model; do not load every numeric row as an indiscriminate set of defaults.

Dry friction 0.8 is the team's toy condition, not validated as peak road friction. Wet 0.35 is both an FHWA stopping-sight-distance design proxy and the reported mean ABS braking coefficient for a specific wet sliding test surface at 30 and 40 km/h. It is not a universal wet-asphalt peak. The dry 0.8 team choice is consistent with dry-asphalt ABS averages of 0.737–0.830 across eight tire sets in the same 2023 experimental study, not an individual measured value for our vehicle. https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/8/6945 The additional dry/wet reference rows evaluate a primary research paper's fitted locked-wheel model at 40 km/h. They are derived estimates for that test surface and wheel condition, **not measured peak ABS friction**, universal bounds, or CARLA tire-friction settings. The wet proxy now has held-out CARLA braking observations. They fit a selected effective target, not a validated wet ABS peak or an empirical rain model.

The 0.7g (6.867 m/s²) hard-brake target is a chosen illustrative dry-condition calibration, not a universal physical maximum. Austin's team definition says hard braking is limited by the road; a wet mu=0.35 input implies the simplified mu*g cap is 3.4335 m/s². Do not apply 6.867 under that cap. The 0.3g soft brake and 200ms actuation delay are protocol assumptions; brake-controller response/buildup in a real experiment is not necessarily the same as a pure dead-time parameter.

CARLA calibration results are from the prior `aiea-auditors` study, not a new `gilpin-lab` run: CARLA 0.9.15, Town04, dry visual weather, dt=0.001s, mass 1845kg, tire-friction scalar=1.0, 1400Nm wheel brake torque (2x stock). Four held-out observations are one deterministic run each; they validate this simulator target fit against declared tolerances, not real-world realism. Details in `../team-lead/calibrated-profile.json`, `../team-lead/brake-calibration.csv`, and `../../docs/GROUNDING_SOURCES.md`. The profile is valid only inside its recorded condition envelope. Wheel friction is not a direct physical mu. Steering-rate, wet-road calibration, exact actor dimensions, and a vehicle-specific measured pure actuator delay remain open.

NHTSA's 5 and 8 km/h crossing-mannequin speeds represent walking and running test conditions; they are not behavioral speed distributions. Existing `local-dart-40` used 2.0 m/s; do not retroactively replace its run configuration.
